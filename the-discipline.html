The Discipline

What Precision Rifle Actually Demands.

Classification, events, and margins — the technical detail behind the discipline.

Classification

SH2

Para shooting sport is divided into two classifications, based on how an athlete stabilises and supports the rifle. SH1 athletes shoot standing or seated without needing external support for the weapon itself. SH2 athletes cannot support the rifle's weight unaided and shoot from a fixed stand — the precision demand is identical; the means of stabilising the platform is different.

Kevin competes SH2. He is currently the only SH2-classified precision rifle athlete in Ireland — there is no domestic peer group, no training partner at the same classification, and no direct comparator on Irish results boards. Every benchmark comes from international competition.

The Events

R4, R5, R9

Three event codes, three disciplines. Each carries a different physical position and a different margin for error.

R4

10m Air Rifle Standing

Fired from 10 metres, rifle supported on a stand rather than the body. The shortest distance of the three, but the least forgiving — no wind, no bullet drop, nothing external to blame. Every deviation traces back to the shooter.

Distance10m

R5

10m Air Rifle Prone

Same distance, prone position. A more stable platform than standing, which raises the expected standard — the margin for error shrinks because the excuse of instability is removed.

Distance10m

R9

50m Smallbore Prone

Fired at five times the distance of R4/R5, with .22 calibre rather than air rifle. Wind reading, light shift, and barrel heat all become live variables — the process has to absorb factors the 10m events don't introduce.

Distance50m

The Margin

What "Precision" Means Here

A 10-ring at 10 metres is roughly half a millimetre across the target's scoring zone once scaled to the pellet's point of impact. At that distance, a barrel movement of a fraction of a degree — invisible to the naked eye — is the difference between a 10 and a 9. There's no recovering a miss mid-shot. The outcome is decided before the trigger breaks, in the stillness and the setup, not in a reaction after the fact.

This is the core reason ZeroPoint exists as a system rather than a habit. A discipline with this little room for correction can't rely on good days — it has to run on a repeatable process that holds regardless of nerves, fatigue, or the size of the moment.
